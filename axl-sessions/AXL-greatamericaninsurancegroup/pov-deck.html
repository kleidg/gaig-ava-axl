Use case 1 · deep dive

Payment status inquiry

Happy path
  1. The IVR has already collected and validated the caller's Tax ID and Bill ID and hands off to the AI experience with that context.
  2. The assistant confirms the bill it's looking at (provider, date of service, amount) so the caller knows it has the right one.
  3. It calls get_bill_payment_status and reads back the status — e.g. "processed on 9/18, payment issued 9/20 for $412.50."
  4. If the caller asks a follow-up ("what's an EOB?"), it answers from Billing Knowledge; otherwise it offers "anything else?" and closes cleanly.
Why it matters
Payment status is the single most common billing call named in your brief — and today each one ties up a Medical Bill Reviewer who also owns fee-schedule audits and dispute work.
Wow moment
A caller gets a specific, dated payment answer — no hold, no transfer — in under 30 seconds, built entirely on the identifiers the IVR already validated.

Use case 2 · deep dive

Documentation & submission status

Happy path
  1. The IVR hands off an already-authenticated caller (Tax ID + Bill ID / claim identifiers validated).
  2. The assistant calls get_documentation_status to check what's on file for the bill or claim.
  3. It reads back what's received and what's outstanding — e.g. "we have the bill and the itemized statement; we're still waiting on the treatment notes."
  4. If something's missing, it offers to send a reminder (with a clear "yes" first) or hands off if the caller wants to submit it another way.
Why it matters
Documentation status is a named top call driver — and today it's a repeat call: a provider checks once, then again after resubmitting, competing with the same reviewer team's audit workload.
Wow moment
The assistant doesn't just say "something's missing" — it names exactly what's outstanding, and can trigger the reminder itself after a confirmation.

Use case 3 · deep dive

Denial & appeal guidance

Happy path
  1. The IVR hands off the authenticated caller with the Bill ID / claim ID already validated.
  2. The assistant calls get_denial_reason and explains the denial code in plain language, plus the appeal steps and deadline from Appeals Knowledge.
  3. If a dispute is already open, it calls get_appeal_status and reads back where it stands.
  4. If the caller wants to file or argue the appeal, it hands off to an Appeals specialist with the denial code and claim already on screen.
Why it matters
Denial and appeal calls are the most emotionally charged and highest-risk of the five drivers named in your brief — getting the reason and status right, without a hold, protects both the caller and the appeals SLA.
Wow moment
The caller hears the specific denial reason and appeal deadline in plain language, then reaches a specialist who already has the denial code and claim loaded — no re-explaining.
